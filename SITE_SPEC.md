# ZoeAnshuman.com - Website Spec

Status: first draft, ready to turn into a wireframe

## 1. The big idea

**A maker's field notebook on the web.**

The site should feel like Zoe opened a slightly messy notebook and invited us to look through the things she is making. It should be fun and handmade, while still making the projects easy to understand and try.

Working headline:

> Hi, I'm Zoe. I make small things that solve real problems.

Working introduction:

> I make apps, websites, and films. Most of them start with something that bothers me - like street animals needing help, Hindi matras being hard to hear, or good food being wasted.

Three words for the design: **curious, useful, alive**.

Three things to avoid: childish, corporate, cluttered.

## 2. What the site needs to do

1. Quickly explain who Zoe is and what she likes to make.
2. Make every project feel real, useful, and easy to try.
3. Show the story connecting the animal projects across two years.
4. Show how Zoe thinks, tests, notices problems, and improves things.
5. Make visitors want to explore instead of reading a formal CV.
6. Live at `https://zoeanshuman.com`.

## 3. Who it is for

- Friends, family, and classmates who want to try the projects.
- Teachers and school council reviewers.
- Other young makers.
- Adults who may be able to help a project grow.

The first version should work without an account, form, or database.

## 4. Page plan

Version 1 is one scrolling home page. This keeps the first build small enough to understand and finish.

### A. Top bar

- Hand-drawn `ZA` mark or tiny paw-print logo.
- Links: `Work`, `How I make`, `Now`.
- A `tidy / doodly` switch. Doodly mode adds stickers, scribbles, and paw prints; tidy mode calms them down. The words and links stay the same.

### B. Hello section

- Large headline: `Hi, I'm Zoe.`
- Short maker statement.
- One friendly photo or illustrated self-portrait.
- Primary button: `See what I've made`.
- Secondary text link: `How I make things`.
- Small live note: `currently adding more helpers to PetzPal`.

### C. The main story: Community animals

Present these as one connected trail, not three unrelated cards:

1. **Through Their Eyes** - a two-minute film written and directed with two friends.
2. **Voice for the Voiceless** - a website explaining how people can help street animals.
3. **PetzPal** - an app that helps people act: find care and keep pet records together.

Visual idea: a winding line with paw-print stops. As the visitor scrolls, each stop opens like a notebook flap.

Each project includes:

- The problem in one sentence.
- What Zoe made.
- A screenshot, short clip, or photo.
- A bright `Try it` or `Watch it` button.
- One honest note about what changed or what is still unfinished.

Special label for PetzPal: `Still growing`.

### D. Two other things I made

Two playful cards:

- **Matra** - hear a matra, record yourself, and compare the sounds.
- **The Kitchen Canvas** - photograph what is in the fridge and find meals that fit, while avoiding allergens.

The cards can tilt very slightly on hover, like notes pinned to a board. They must sit still when reduced motion is enabled.

### E. How I make things

Use Zoe's real process as a four-step doodle:

`Notice something annoying -> make a small version -> watch someone use it -> fix where they get stuck`

Supporting line:

> None of these are drawings of apps. They all actually work.

### F. Now / learning shelf

A small, changeable section with three notes:

- `Building:` the current project or improvement.
- `Learning:` one coding or design skill.
- `Wondering:` one question Zoe wants to explore.

This gives people a reason to visit again without needing a full blog.

### G. Off the keyboard

A compact personality section showing what Zoe enjoys when she is not building. Working title: `Things I can happily talk about for hours`.

Use four scrapbook-style tiles:

- **Tennis** - `I play tennis.` A tennis-ball doodle can roll a few centimetres when the card is opened.
- **Formula 1** - `I watch, analyse, and geek out over F1 - especially Lewis Hamilton and Ferrari.` Give this tile a tiny timing-screen or race-strategy-note feeling, without copying official team branding.
- **Cricket** - `I follow Virat Kohli and the Indian women's cricket team.` Treat both interests equally rather than making the women's team a small footnote.
- **Travel** - `15 countries and counting.` Show a playful collection of made-up passport-style stamps or a scratch-map pattern, not a live location map.

These should reveal personality, not become full sports-stat pages. Avoid live scores in version 1 because they add technical complexity and quickly become outdated.

Optional rotating line near this section:

> Usually building something, hitting a tennis ball, watching a race, following a match, or planning the next adventure.

### H. Footer

- Short ending: `Thanks for looking around my notebook.`
- A parent-approved contact link, if wanted.
- No school name, exact age, personal phone number, or private email address.
- Tiny easter egg: clicking the paw logo makes one animal walk across the footer.

## 5. Project card recipe

Every project should answer the same six questions:

1. What bothered me?
2. What did I make?
3. Who did I make it with?
4. Can I try it?
5. What did I learn from someone using it?
6. What would I improve next?

This makes the portfolio stronger than a list of technologies. It shows thinking.

## 6. Look and feel

### Visual direction

- Warm paper background rather than pure white.
- Ink-like dark navy text.
- Deep green from the current portfolio for the main structure.
- Tangerine orange for links and active moments.
- One surprise accent, such as lilac or bright sky blue, for stickers.
- Clean sans-serif for labels; friendly serif for stories and notes.
- Rounded project images, thin pencil lines, tape, stamps, and small handwritten annotations.

### Quirky details

- Paw prints connect the animal-project timeline.
- `Tidy / doodly` changes the decoration level.
- Buttons use human labels such as `Try the real thing`, not `Learn more`.
- A small stamp can say `made, tested, changed`.
- Small visual references can appear across the page: a tennis-ball circle, an F1-style timing strip, a cricket scorecard line, and travel stamps.
- The 404 page can say `This page wandered off` and guide visitors home.

### Rules that keep the fun useful

- No autoplaying sound or video.
- No custom cursor that makes clicking harder.
- No long opening animation.
- No chatbot in version 1.
- All effects work with a keyboard and stop when a visitor prefers reduced motion.
- Project words and buttons always stay more noticeable than the decoration.

## 7. What we are borrowing - and what we are not

From `ana.sh`:

- Borrow the personal dashboard feeling, small changing status, and sense that hobbies belong beside serious work.
- Do not copy the grid, logo, colours, or exact cards.

From `suhailroushan.com`:

- Borrow the strong handmade idea and the playful ability to switch presentation modes.
- Do not copy the notebook photograph, dark developer theme, or exact mode switch.

Zoe's version is a field notebook about noticing real-world problems and making working responses.

## 9. Privacy and safety defaults

Because this is a public site for a young maker:

- Say `student maker` instead of publishing an exact age or grade.
- Do not publish the school name.
- Do not publish a home address, routine, phone number, or personal email.
- Remove location information from uploaded photos before publishing when possible.
- Do not publish future travel dates, hotel details, flight details, or a live location.
- Use a parent-managed email or no contact link at all.
- Do not add visitor tracking in version 1.

## 10. Build plan for learning

Use **Vite with plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript** for version 1.

Why: it is small and fast, and Zoe can see what each language does instead of hiding the basics behind a large framework.

Learning path:

1. HTML: put the story and project sections on the page.
2. CSS: make the notebook layout, colours, spacing, and responsive cards.
3. JavaScript: add the tidy/doodly switch and the small footer easter egg.
4. Git: save each meaningful step as a commit.
5. Hosting: publish the site, then connect the custom domain.

Suggested first Git commits:

- `chore: start portfolio site`
- `feat: add Zoe introduction and project stories`
- `style: create field notebook design`
- `feat: add tidy and doodly modes`
- `fix: improve mobile and keyboard use`

## 11. Domain plan

The final home should be the root address: `https://zoeanshuman.com/`.

Current observation on September 14, 2026: the domain does not resolve yet, and this repository is empty with no commits or remote. That means this is a fresh launch rather than a risky replacement.

Launch steps:

1. Build and test the site locally.
2. Create the first Git commits.
3. Connect the repository to a static hosting service.
4. Add the host's DNS records where the domain is registered.
5. Make both `zoeanshuman.com` and `www.zoeanshuman.com` work.
6. Redirect `www` to the shorter main address.
7. Confirm HTTPS is active.
8. Test every live project link on phone and computer.

The projects can stay on their current app addresses for now. The portfolio becomes their main doorway.

## 12. Version 1 acceptance checklist

The first version is finished when:

- The page clearly introduces Zoe within the first screen.
- All five projects appear and have working links.
- The three animal projects read as one connected story.
- The site works at 320px phone width and on a normal laptop.
- Keyboard users can reach every link and control.
- Text remains readable with JavaScript turned off.
- Motion turns off with `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Images have useful alt text and do not cause layout jumps.
- No private school, age, location, or contact information is exposed.
- The root domain and `www` address use HTTPS.
- A custom 404 page helps visitors return home.
